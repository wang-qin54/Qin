Asset redesign

Role
Product designer — information architecture, prioritisation, interaction
Product
Crypto & US-stock trading app · iOS / Android
Team
One PM, ahead of engineering
Timeline
One week to alignment · three months in production

A redesign of the asset page — the home of the Asset tab — for a platform where people trade both crypto and US stocks. The project had been stuck for almost two years. When it finally moved, the brief was one line: “it looks messy.” My job was to turn that into a clear problem, a shippable hierarchy, and a decision the team could align on in a week. Three months later, after a product pivot and a design-system migration I didn’t control, the structure was still standing — the part of this case I trust most.

01 · Problem & diagnosis

A two-year-stuck screen, a fast clock, and a one-line brief: “it looks messy”

  • Stuck for almost two years. When it finally moved, the bar was: ship fast, with limited resources, no compromise on the experience.
  • A vague brief, one concrete ask. Leadership said the account overview “looked messy” but couldn’t say what was wrong — and wanted two split holdings sections merged so people could tell what they owned.
  • Design was the gate. Engineering hadn’t started; nothing would be built until leadership agreed on a direction. I had to produce a call the team could commit to — not a vision that would stall again.

02 · Constraints & trade-offs

Why there was no template to copy

Three forces on one small screen. The obvious references — the top crypto exchanges and the established brokerage apps — had each solved for one of them, and none of their answers transferred.

  1. 1

    Multi-account, surfaced not buried

    Other exchanges support multiple accounts, but keep the switcher inside account settings — reasonable when most people only hold one. Ours were high-net-worth users running several at once, so the switcher had to sit on the asset page itself and drive every figure below it.

  2. 2

    Two pattern languages, one screen

    Crypto apps and brokerages have trained their users on different conventions. Most products pick a side; this one carried both asset classes from day one, so neither pattern could simply win — the page had to read as familiar arriving from either habit.

  3. 3

    A new product still explaining itself

    The incumbents can afford calm, spacious screens — nobody needs telling what they do. A new product has no such luxury: this page also had to carry the case for what made it different, without sliding back into the density we were there to fix.

Every decision was a trade-off

A management review to clear, and no engineering resourced yet — none of the ideal answers were free.

Chose hierarchy within the existing data model — shippable in two weeks, no new APIs.

ChoseOne structure across crypto & securities

Chose one structure, and moved crypto to meet it. Securities ran on a third-party interface that was expensive to change, so the alignment had to come from the crypto side — a grey summary card above holdings carrying total assets, borrowed amount, and the other account-level figures, so both asset classes read the same way.

Chose a calm home — protect the reading path; defer financing offers.

Decision 02, in three generations

“One card per account, with a Crypto / Securities switch” is the conclusion. It took three builds to get there, and each one failed at something specific.

  • 1Where the switch lives — Gen 1 selects by tapping a card; Gen 2 moves it to a segmented control; Gen 3 keeps the control and folds the accounts under the number.
  • 2What the panel holds — Gen 1 stacks rings and bars; Gen 2 fits the real fields; Gen 3 makes the account split an expandable line that adds up to the headline.
  • 3What was outside the panel — Gen 2 dropped the account cards below; Gen 3 brings them inside — and the credit line, new since the pivot, sits where borrowing used to.

04 · How I worked

One week, a clickable prototype almost every day

The differentiator wasn’t just speed — it was the medium. Every day, stakeholders reviewed a clickable HTML prototype with realistic data, not a static Figma frame. They could feel the hierarchy, tap the account switch, and argue about a real experience. That compressed two years of ambiguity into one week of decisions.

The ambitious end, built to be clicked

Before locking the shippable direction I built the ambitious end of the design space as a working prototype — not a leftover ideal, but the cheapest way to make the cost of each trade-off concrete for the people deciding.

  • Net worth, not just total. Led with total assets − borrowed and a 30-day trend — the number high-net-worth users actually track.
  • Multi-account aggregation. Combine selected accounts into one net-worth, borrowed, and buying-power view — the multi-account constraint, solved directly.
  • Asset-class breakdown. Crypto and securities in one proportional read, each expandable, so both kinds of trader find their world fast.
  • Financing & liquidity. Surfaced “how much can I borrow,” buying power, and one-tap repay — the marketing ask, made genuinely useful.
What exploration bought us Prototyping the ambitious end made the cost of each trade-off concrete — and let the team choose the shippable hierarchy with confidence, in one week of alignment and two of build.
