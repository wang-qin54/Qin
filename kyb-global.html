02 · Problem

The brief asked for a selector; the drop-off sat on documents every company uploads

A research report flagged low KYB conversion, and a feature request came with it. Before designing it, I went back to the report's funnel.

  • Where companies dropped: of 114 that submitted documents, 47 were approved, or 41%.
  • Why a selector would miss: the biggest drop sat on documents nearly every company type uploads, so filtering fields by type wouldn't reach it.
  • Two patterns: some submitted without reading the requirements and were rejected; others stopped at the upload step.

04 · Approach

Benchmarks were an input; the data decided each option's role

Binance, OKX, Coinbase and Kraken share three patterns: pick the entity type first, show a document checklist up front, and write requirements in plain language. I tested each against what the data showed.

Institutional verification flows at leading exchanges.

Three options, three roles
Option A · kept for scale
Company-type selector

Rejections came from comprehension, not irrelevant fields, so a selector wouldn't move today's numbers. I kept it to route each market's document set as EU and AU rules diverge.

Option B · supporting role
Help text

Users already skipped long text, so help text only explains terms; it never carries a rule.

Option C · core fix
Guided required choice

With the rule in text, 67% of failed applications got this document wrong, so the choice became a required step that leads straight to the right document.

05 · Solution

Rules users missed became a choice or a visible order

The first fix targets the board document (wrong in 67% of failed applications), the second the shareholder list (24%). The third tells companies up front that compliance may ask for more.

1 · Board or no board becomes a required choice

Designed as required with nothing pre-selected, so users must pick their structure first. The form then shows only the document that applies, with its template.

Before: the rule was one line under the upload. After: the choice comes first.

2 · Shareholder rules in a visible order

What the register must include sits in a highlighted list, and the 25% conditions become two options instead of a paragraph to decode.

Before: two or three requirements mixed into the description. After: each one visible.

3 · A place for extra documents

An optional last step tells companies compliance may ask for more, and lets them add it up front.

Before: no hint that more documents might follow. After: an optional additional-documents step.

06 · Edge cases

Every rejection and request comes with a reason and a next step

Institutional applications rarely pass in one go, so the states after submission matter as much as the form.

More documents requested

High-risk applicants are asked for more after submission, each item with its reason, so the request doesn't feel like a dead end.

Rejected, with reasons

A rejection lists specific, fixable reasons, so companies can correct and resubmit instead of guessing.

Continue later

Institutions need time to gather documents, so progress is saved and the flow resumes where they left off.

08 · Result

Pass rate moved one point; the pattern became the base for two new markets

Both fixes shipped, but in the build the board choice came with a default already selected, so companies could move on without choosing and the core mechanism didn't run as designed. The first-time review pass rate for submitted applications went from 41% to 42%, well short of what we expected, so I took the rejections back to customer support.

First market
  • Shareholder hierarchy shipped as designed
  • Board choice shipped with a default, not as a required choice
  • First-time review pass rate: 41% → 42%
EU and AU
  • The guided pattern became the base for both institutional flows
  • Part of the EU and AU licence submissions

KYB EU and AU: the same pattern extended to multi-layer ownership, beneficial-owner thresholds and local source-of-funds rules. Full screens on request.

09 · Reflection

Most remaining rejections weren't on the screen

I went through the rejections one by one with customer support and compliance. Most companies had picked the right document and still got it back.

Validity rules
Rules the screen never showed

Stamped certified copies, a hand-signed POA, the company name on the register, and original ID photos rather than screenshots.

Unneeded asks
Requirements compliance didn't review

A hand-held ID with a written signature that no one checked, and ECDD asked of every company when only two entity types need it.

What I'd do next time
1
Show validity rules where the file is chosen

Stamp, original and signature rules belong at the upload, not in a help article.

2
Write the key interaction into acceptance criteria

“Required, no default” lived only in the mockups and was easy to lose in the build. It belongs in the acceptance criteria and a design QA pass before release.

3
Read rejections with support before designing

One session with the people who handle rejections shows early which causes sit in policy and the back end.
