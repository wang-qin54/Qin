02 · How to use

A new case needs three shared files and one way of writing

Every block is opt-in by class, and its styles live in shared files, not in the page. Start a new case from the skeleton below and use only blocks from this page. When a case needs something new, add it to the shared files and to this page first, then use it.

Page skeleton
new-case.html
DoCopy this skeleton, keep the load order, number sections 02 onward (the hero is 01).
AvoidPage-level <style> copies of shared blocks; a page prefix (kyb-, aml-) for a block that exists here.

Story arc
section order

One case tells one story. Order sections by the reader's questions: what happened, what I owned, what was wrong, what I changed, what it led to, what I learned.

  1. Hero
  2. In short
  3. Context and role
  4. Research and problem
  5. Fixes 1–3
  6. Result
  7. Reflection

6–10 sections; a reader who scans only the headings still gets the argument.

04 · Section rhythm

Every section: kicker, claim heading, lead, then one block

The kicker names the section; the heading says what changed. The lead repeats the heading's key terms and sets up the block that follows.

Section header
.cs-kicker + h3.section + p
DoWrite the heading as a claim a skimmer can follow ("Watch agents first, then redesign").
AvoidLabel headings ("Research", "Final delivery"), slogans, number riddles.

05 · Text blocks

Lists, callouts and optional depth

Keep body copy short. Use a callout for the one insight a section turns on; fold detail that only some readers want.

Bullet and check lists
.cs-list · .cs-list--check
DoBullets for facts; checks only for real outcomes. Bold the first words sparingly.
AvoidChecks on plans or unmeasured claims; more than 5 items.
  • 5 agents, 2–3 per session, on the live MVP
  • Every point of confusion pinned to the screen, then grouped into three blockers
  • Five platforms built from scratch in six months
  • Not fully rolled out: the team was restructured before the pilot
Callout
.cs-callout
DoOne per section at most: the key insight, a user quote, or the decision. Grey note, 2px accent rule on the left, square left corners, rounded right.
AvoidTwo callouts in a row; a callout that repeats the lead; a tinted blue fill or a thick rule inside fully rounded corners.
Key finding

4 of 5 agents didn't realise they had to take a ticket before replying.

Decision
One focused change per blocker.
Scoped to fit the development deadline
Disclosure
details.cs-more
DoFold method detail, extra screens or edge cases; say what's inside in the hint.
AvoidHiding the main argument or the result.
How the sessions ran5 agents · 2–3 per session

Agents worked on the live MVP. We pinned every point of confusion to the screen, then grouped them into three structural blockers.

06 · Cards and steps

Cards for parallel points, steps for a sequence

Use cards for 2–4 parallel points, numbered tiles when order matters, and a pill flow when the reader only needs to scan a sequence.

Card grid
.cs-cards · .cs-card · --cols
Do2–4 cards, one idea each: label, title, one sentence. style="--cols:3" for three; .cs-card--num for numbered, .cs-card--soft for a borderless grey grid.
AvoidParagraphs inside cards; mixing numbered and unnumbered cards in one grid.

08 · Screens

Every description of a screen points at the place it describes

Screens are exported from Figma at 3×. Use a plain shot with a caption, an annotated screen for several notes, and a before/after card for one fix.

Screenshot
figure.cs-shot + p.cs-figcap
DoWidth/height = exported pixels; caption names what to look at.
AvoidDescribing a detail without marking it; export sizes in captions.

The redesigned workspace: ticket, chat and context on one screen.

Annotated screen
figure.db-anno
Do3–5 pins at % positions; notes are a 2–4 word keyword + one line. Hover links pin and note.
AvoidMore than 5 pins; split into two screens or crop.
Before / after card
.db-wt > article.db-wt__card
DoOne fix per card. Before caption above its image, After caption below. Boxes in % hug the control. Optional evidence pill (.db-wt__pill, e.g. "4 of 5 agents") only for observed counts.

09 · Result and reflection

Results state real outcomes; reflections state practices

The summary opens the case; result panels and reflection cards close it. With no usage data, a one-line note replaces the numbers.

Story summary
.cs-sum · .is-turn
DoFirst section ("In short"): 3–4 rows; .is-turn on the row the case is about.
AvoidPutting it in the hero; more than one sentence per row.
Problem

4 of 5 agents didn't know they had to take a ticket first.

What I did

Led 2 designers through three focused fixes in two weeks.

Result

Ready for a 20-agent pilot, then the team was restructured.

Result panels
.cs-compare > .cs-panel
DoTwo scopes side by side, 2–4 checks each, then p.cs-note if metrics are missing.
AvoidTwo rows of panels; unmeasured claims as checks.

Reflection cards
.cs-learns > .cs-learn
Do3–4 lessons, title as an action, one sentence each; strongest point first.
Avoid"Failed", "pivot", "broke"; generic lessons ("communication matters").
1
Match designers to the problem

Assigning work by each designer's strength, such as interaction logic or visual design, avoids costly redesigns later.

2

10 · Checklist

Check before shipping a case

Full rules are in case-library.mdc.

  1. Facts. Every number, title and claim matches career-facts.mdc; no unmeasured results.
  2. Skim test. Reading only the hero and the section headings tells the whole story.
  3. One block per idea. Each section has a lead and at most one callout; no stat tiles repeat the hero.
  4. Shared blocks only. Blocks come from this library; the page's own <style> holds only what the case invents.
  5. Screens. 3× exports, width/height set to exported pixels, every described detail marked.
  6. Both languages. Every text node has data-zh; checked in EN and 中文 at desktop and phone width.
  7. Cache. ?v= bumped on every changed CSS, JS and image.
